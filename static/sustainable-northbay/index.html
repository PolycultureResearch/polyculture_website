How businesses get on this map

Every business on this map is here because of a verifiable sustainability commitment.

The five kinds of evidence

These are ordered by how independent the evidence is from the business itself. That is not the same as how strong it is. A ranch running an independent ecological monitoring program is better evidenced than a company that bought an audit.

Third-party certified. An outside body assessed the business and can take the certification away. B Corp, Regenerative Organic Certified, Demeter Biodynamic, Climate Neutral, science-based targets validated by SBTi, LEED, Fair Trade, FSC, Sun+Earth, the California Green Business Network. Most of the businesses on this map are certified in some independent way.

Measured outcomes. The business collects or publishes actual measurement of its environmental effect, ideally with a named independent partner. This is rarer than certification and, when it is real, it is more transparent and can be more rigorous.

Structural. The commitment is written into the ownership or a legal instrument, so it survives a change of management. Worker cooperatives, ESOPs, benefit corporations, social purpose corporations, nonprofit ownership, conservation easements, land trusts, and land held by a conservation organization and farmed by an independent operator. Seventeen businesses on this map are structurally committed to a mission, and most of them are also certified. We flag it separately because it is a different kind of durability: a certification can lapse when the person who championed it leaves, but a deed to the land trust can not.

Documented practice. Specific operational facts the business states publicly and anyone can check: certified inputs, packaging, on-site energy, sourcing.

Locally attested. A named person vouches for a practice that is real but not documented anywhere public yet. We say who is vouching and when. This is the weakest tier.

What it takes to get listed

If you'd like to nominate a business to be included on the map, please let us know. Be sure you describe sustainability commitment. These are some good questions for thinking about the sustainability claims:

Is it material? Compostable cups at a coffee shop are material. Compostable cups at a machine shop are decoration. The practice has to touch what the business actually does.

Is it specific? A number, a named certifier, a named supplier, a named partner. The claim has to be specific enough to be falsifiable.

Is it current? Certifications lapse, companies get acquired, and sustainability programs leave with the person who ran them.

What does not qualify

Having an ESG page. A sustainability section on the website with no specifics behind it. Awards the business gave itself. Membership in something with no assessment attached. In this region specifically, more than 1,100 entities hold Certified California Sustainable Winegrowing and roughly a quarter of Napa's wineries hold Napa Green, so neither is on its own a sign of leadership, and the standards are too low to mean very much.
